Shopify SEO for beginners: how to get your store found on Google

By the Shoply Apps team · 20 October 2026 · 9 min read

Key takeaways

  • Shopify SEO means helping Google crawl, index and understand your store; Shopify creates a sitemap at /sitemap.xml and a default robots.txt for you.
  • Set up Google Search Console, verify your domain and submit sitemap.xml so you can see which pages are indexed and which searches find you.
  • Give each page one main keyword that matches search intent, favour specific long-tail phrases, and treat collection pages as your main SEO landing pages.
  • Write a clear homepage title and meta description, use original product text and image alt text, keep the store fast on mobile and earn links rather than buying them.
  • SEO takes months, not days: a 30-day plan gets the foundations right, but no one can guarantee rankings.

Shopify SEO (search engine optimisation) is the work of helping Google find, understand and show your store's pages when people search for what you sell. Shopify handles some technical basics for you, such as a sitemap and a robots.txt file, but the parts that move the needle are yours: choosing the right keywords, writing clear titles and descriptions, building useful collection pages and earning trust over time. This guide covers store-wide Shopify SEO for beginners, with a 30-day plan at the end.

One honest note before you start: SEO is slow. New stores often wait several months before seeing steady search traffic, and nobody can promise you a ranking. What you can control is making every page clear, useful and easy for Google to read.

How does Google find a Shopify store?

Google finds your store by crawling it, then decides whether to add each page to its index, and only indexed pages can appear in search results. Crawling means Google's bot visits a page and follows its links to other pages. Indexing means Google stores the page and what it is about so it can show it for relevant searches.

Shopify helps with both automatically:

  • Sitemap. Shopify generates a sitemap for your store at yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml. A sitemap is a file that lists your pages so search engines can find them.
  • robots.txt. Every Shopify store has a default robots.txt file at yourdomain.com/robots.txt. It tells crawlers which areas to skip, such as the cart and checkout. Most beginners should leave it as it is.

One thing to check: if your online store is still password protected, search engines cannot see it. Remove the password when you are ready to launch.

How do you set up Google Search Console for Shopify?

Google Search Console is a free Google tool that shows which searches bring people to your site, which pages are indexed and what problems Google finds. Setting it up takes three general steps:

  1. Add your store as a property. Sign in to Search Console with a Google account and add your store's domain. A domain property covers every version of your site (with and without www).
  2. Verify that you own it. For a domain property, Google gives you a TXT record to add to your domain's DNS settings, wherever your domain is managed. Another option is a URL-prefix property verified with an HTML meta tag added to your theme. Follow Google's on-screen instructions.
  3. Submit your sitemap. In the Sitemaps section, enter sitemap.xml and submit it. Google will then use it to discover your pages.

Once data appears, check the Pages report for pages that are not indexed, and the Performance report to see the search terms people actually use to find you.

How do you choose keywords for a small Shopify store?

Choose keywords that match what your customers type and what each page actually offers, and favour specific long-tail phrases over broad ones. A keyword is simply the search phrase you want a page to show up for.

Understand search intent

Search intent is the reason behind a search. Someone typing "how to clean a cast iron pan" wants information; someone typing "cast iron skillet 10 inch" probably wants to buy. Google tends to show pages that match the intent, so put buying keywords on product and collection pages and question keywords on blog posts.

Go long-tail

Long-tail keywords are longer, more specific phrases such as "vegan leather laptop sleeve 14 inch" rather than "laptop sleeve". Each one gets fewer searches, but there is usually less competition from big retailers, and the people searching know exactly what they want. For a new store, long-tail keywords are the realistic place to start.

For ideas, use Google's autocomplete suggestions, the words customers use in reviews and emails, and the queries Search Console shows you.

One main keyword per page

Give each page one main keyword, plus a few close variations. If two pages target the same phrase, they compete with each other and Google has to guess which one to show. A simple keyword map keeps this straight. Here is an example for a made-up candle store:

PageMain keywordIntent
Homepagehandmade soy candlesBuying / brand
Collection: Scented candlesscented soy candlesBuying
Collection: Candle gift setscandle gift setBuying
Product: Lavender jar candlelavender soy candleBuying

Blog postsoy wax vs paraffin candlesInformation / comparison

A worked example of the effort involved: say your store has 1 homepage, 6 collections, 40 products and 3 blog posts. That is 1 + 6 + 40 + 3 = 50 pages to map. If you map and update 5 pages a day, you finish in 10 working days. Start with the homepage and collections.

How do you write your homepage title and meta description?

Write a homepage title that says what you sell and your store name, and a meta description that gives a searcher a reason to click. You set both in your Shopify admin under Online Store > Preferences.

  • Title tag: the clickable headline in search results. Keep it to roughly 50–60 characters so it is less likely to be cut off, for example "Handmade Soy Candles and Gift Sets | Ember & Wick".
  • Meta description: the short summary under the title. Aim for roughly 150–160 characters that describe what you sell and what makes you different, such as free returns or small-batch production, but only if it is true. Google sometimes rewrites descriptions, but a good one still helps.

Why are collection pages so important for Shopify SEO?

Collection pages often make the best SEO landing pages in a Shopify store, because they match broad buying searches like "linen summer dresses" better than any single product does.

To turn a collection into a landing page:

  • Give it a clear title that uses its keyword, such as "Linen Summer Dresses" rather than "New Drop 3".
  • Add a short intro in the collection description: two to four sentences on what is in the collection and who it is for. Keep it helpful and readable, not stuffed with keywords.
  • Edit the search engine listing at the bottom of the collection page in your admin to set its page title, meta description and a clean URL handle.

Product pages need the same care at a smaller scale. Rather than repeat it here, use our Shopify product page SEO checklist for product titles, descriptions and alt text.

How should you structure your store and internal links?

Keep your structure shallow and logical: homepage, then collections, then products, so any product is a few clicks from the homepage. Internal links are links from one page of your store to another; they show shoppers and Google which pages matter.

  • Put your main collections in the main menu.
  • Link from blog posts to the relevant collections and products.
  • Use descriptive link text such as "see our scented soy candles" instead of "click here".
  • Make sure every product belongs to at least one collection, so it is not an orphan page with no links pointing to it.

How do you avoid duplicate content on Shopify?

Duplicate content means the same or very similar content appearing at more than one URL. On Shopify, a product can be reached both at /products/product-name and through a collection path. Shopify themes typically add a canonical tag, a line of code that tells Google which URL is the main version, so this is usually handled for you.

The duplicates you do control are in your content:

  • Do not paste the same supplier description onto many products, or onto products that other stores sell with identical text. Write your own.
  • Do not create several collections that hold the same products under slightly different names.

Does page speed, mobile and image alt text matter?

Yes. Google looks at how pages perform for real visitors, and most shoppers browse on their phones, so a slow or awkward mobile store hurts both rankings and sales.

  • Speed: compress large images before uploading, remove apps you no longer use (some leave code behind in your theme), and avoid stacking sliders and pop-ups. Check pages with Google's PageSpeed Insights and the Core Web Vitals report in Search Console.
  • Mobile: open your store on your own phone and try to find a product, read its details and add it to the cart. Our guide on increasing your Shopify conversion rate covers more of this.
  • Alt text: a short written description of an image, read by screen readers and used by Google to understand the picture. Describe what is in the photo, for example "lavender soy candle in amber glass jar".

Should you start a blog on your Shopify store?

A blog is worth it if you can publish genuinely useful posts regularly, because it lets you rank for question searches that product pages cannot. Shopify has a built-in blog feature, so you do not need a separate platform.

Content ideas that tend to work for small stores:

  • How-to guides for using or caring for what you sell.
  • Buying guides and size or fit guides.
  • Comparisons, such as two materials or two styles.
  • Answers to the questions customers email you most.

One thorough post a month that answers a real question beats several thin ones. Link each post to the collections it relates to.

How do you get other websites to link to your store?

Earn links by giving people a real reason to mention you. Links from trusted sites act like recommendations. For a small store, realistic sources are:

  • Suppliers and brands you stock, which may have a "where to buy" or stockists page.
  • Local press and blogs, especially if you have a local story, event or collaboration.
  • Partners such as makers you work with, complementary shops or community groups you support.
  • Useful content, like a detailed guide others want to reference.

Do not buy links or join link schemes. Google's guidelines treat them as spam, and they can hurt your store rather than help it.

How do you get found in AI search?

AI search tools, such as Google's AI-generated answers and chat assistants, tend to draw on pages that state facts clearly. The same habits that help normal SEO help here:

  • Write clear, factual product and collection pages: materials, sizes, what is included, shipping and returns.
  • Add FAQs that answer real customer questions in plain sentences.
  • Keep your business information consistent everywhere: store name, contact details and policies should match on your site, social profiles and any listings.

What is a realistic 30-day Shopify SEO plan for beginners?

Spread the work over four weeks, foundations first. This plan will not make you rank in 30 days, but it gets your store into good shape so it can start building visibility over the following months.

WeekFocusTasks
Week 1FoundationsRemove the store password if you are live, set up Google Search Console, verify your domain, submit sitemap.xml, write your homepage title and meta description.
Week 2Keywords and collectionsBuild a keyword map, rename unclear collections, add intro text and search engine listings to your main collections.
Week 3Products and imagesWork through product titles, descriptions, SEO fields and alt text, starting with best sellers; fix copied supplier text.
Week 4Structure and contentTidy menus and internal links, test speed and mobile, publish your first helpful blog post, list possible link sources.

After that, check Search Console once a week, publish regularly and review your product pages once a month as part of a wider catalog health checklist.

FAQ

How long does Shopify SEO take to work?

It usually takes several months before a new store sees steady search traffic, and results vary by niche and competition. Fix the foundations first, then keep improving pages and publishing useful content.

Does Shopify create a sitemap automatically?

Yes. Shopify generates a sitemap at yourdomain.com/sitemap.xml and updates it as you add products, collections, pages and blog posts. Submit it in Google Search Console so Google can find your pages.

Do I need an app for Shopify SEO?

No. You can do the essentials in your Shopify admin: page titles, meta descriptions, URL handles, alt text and blog posts. Apps can save time on large catalogs, but they do not replace clear, useful content.

Should I buy backlinks for my Shopify store?

No. Google treats paid links meant to manipulate rankings as spam, and they can harm your store. Earn links from suppliers, partners, local press and useful content instead.
