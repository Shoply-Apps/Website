How to sell on Instagram with Shopify: setup and posts that convert

By the Shoply Apps team · 20 October 2026 · 10 min read

Key takeaways

  • To sell on Instagram with Shopify, switch to a professional account, connect your catalog through Shopify's Facebook & Instagram sales channel, and tag products once your shop is approved; eligibility depends on your country and Meta's commerce policies, so check the current requirements.
  • Your Shopify product data syncs to Instagram, so clear titles, good images, correct prices and accurate stock matter as much there as on your store.
  • Reels and collabs mostly bring reach; carousels, Stories, product tags and DMs mostly bring sales, so use both kinds of post.
  • A post that converts shows the product in use, answers the main objection, states the price or offer, gives a reason to act and makes buying one tap away.
  • Measure with Instagram's insights plus Shopify sales by channel and UTM-tagged links, and judge any ads by cost per order against your margin.

You can sell on Instagram with Shopify by connecting your store's product catalog to Instagram, so people can tap a product in your posts and buy it on your site. This guide covers the setup and the posts that turn scrollers into buyers, with a caption formula, a format table and a worked launch-week example.

How do you set up Instagram Shopping with Shopify?

You need a professional Instagram account, a Meta business setup and Shopify's Facebook & Instagram sales channel. A sales channel is a place outside your online store where Shopify can list and sell your products; this one is built by Meta and syncs your products to a catalog that Instagram can use.

  1. Switch to a professional account. In Instagram's settings, change your account type to business or creator. A professional account unlocks insights (your post and audience statistics) and shopping features.
  2. Add the Facebook & Instagram channel in Shopify. Find it in the Shopify App Store or under sales channels in your admin, then follow the steps to connect your Meta business account, Facebook Page and Instagram account.
  3. Choose which products to sync. You can share all products or only some collections. Start with the products you are ready to promote.
  4. Wait for review. Meta reviews shops before shopping features turn on. Once approved, you can tag products in your content.

Shopping features, who qualifies and where they are available depend on your country and on Meta's commerce policies, and they change. Check Meta's and Shopify's current requirements before you plan around a specific feature. If tagging is not available to you, a link in bio and link stickers in Stories still work.

Why does product data quality matter here?

Your catalog syncs from Shopify, so whatever is in your product data is what shoppers see when they tap a tag. Before you sync, check titles, descriptions and images with our product page SEO checklist and make photos consistent with the product image size guide.

How should you set up your Instagram profile to sell?

Your profile should tell a new visitor in a few seconds what you sell, who it is for and where to buy.

  • Bio: what you sell, for whom and what makes you different, for example "Linen shirts for hot summers, made to last".
  • Link in bio: point it to a collection or a simple landing page, not just your home page. Add UTM tracking to the link, for example ?utm_source=instagram&utm_medium=social&utm_campaign=bio. UTM parameters are tags on a URL that tell your analytics where a visit came from.
  • Highlights: saved Stories pinned to your profile. Useful ones are Shipping, Returns, Reviews and How to wear or How to use. They answer the questions that stop people from buying.

Which Instagram post formats sell best?

No single format does everything: some formats are better for reaching new people, others for turning followers into buyers. A healthy mix uses reach formats to bring people in and conversion formats to close the sale.

What makes an Instagram post convert into sales?

A post converts when it shows someone why they want the product, removes their main doubt and makes buying easy right now. Check each selling post against this list:

  • Product in use. Show it worn, used or in a real setting, not only on a white background.
  • The main objection answered. Price, fit, quality, shipping time? Pick the biggest one and address it.
  • A clear price or offer. People scroll past products when they have to hunt for the price.
  • A reason to act. A new drop, limited sizes, a seasonal need or a genuine deadline. Never invent fake scarcity.
  • A way to buy. A product tag, a link sticker or "link in bio" with a matching collection.

A 5-part caption formula

Hook → Benefit → Objection answered → Offer → Call to action

Here is an example for a fictional store called Linen & Lane:

Too hot for your favourite shirt? (hook)
Our Coast linen shirt stays cool and gets softer every wash. (benefit)
Worried about wrinkles? It is cut relaxed, so creases look intentional, and the fabric is pre-washed so it will not shrink. (objection)
$59 in five colours, with free returns within 30 days. (offer)
Tap the tag to find your size. Sand and Olive are low in medium. (call to action)

Keep the first line strong, since longer captions are cut off behind a "more" link. The fuller your product page, the more these clicks turn into orders; our guide to increasing your Shopify conversion rate covers what happens after the tap.

How do you use Stories and DMs to sell?

Stories and direct messages (DMs) are where followers who are already interested ask questions and decide, so treat them as part of your checkout.

Stories that sell

Interactive stickers commonly available in Stories include:

  • Polls: "Sand or Olive?" gets people choosing, and the result tells you what to stock.
  • Questions: collect objections, then answer them in the next Stories.
  • Link stickers: send people straight to a product or collection, with UTM tags.
  • Countdowns: remind people of a launch or the end of a real offer.

DMs as a sales channel

A message like "Does this run small?" is a buyer with one question left. Answer every product question, ideally the same day. Use the quick replies or saved answers your account offers for common questions on sizing, shipping and returns, then add a personal line and a link to the product. Never leave a question unanswered.

How do you use customer photos and reposts?

User-generated content (UGC) means photos and videos made by your customers, and it is persuasive because it shows real people using the product.

Always ask for permission before reposting, credit the creator, and keep a record of their yes, such as a screenshot of the message. If you pay or gift products to creators, many countries require the post to be clearly disclosed as an ad or partnership; check the rules where you and your audience are. Our influencer marketing guide covers finding, paying and measuring creators.

Should you run Instagram ads for your Shopify store?

Run ads only after you have posts that already sell without them, then start small and measure cost per order against your margin. Boosting a proven post is the simplest way to start, because you already know the content works.

  • Pick a post with good clicks or orders from your normal followers.
  • Test a small budget for a few days first.
  • Compare cost per order with the profit per order. If an order earns you $20 after product cost, shipping and fees, an ad that costs $25 per order loses money.

Work out your real profit per order first with our guide to profit margins and pricing.

How do you measure sales from Instagram?

Use Instagram's insights for what happened on Instagram, and Shopify's analytics for what happened in your store. Insights show reach (how many accounts saw your content), profile visits, link taps and, where available, product tag taps. Shopify's reports show sessions and sales by channel and referrer, and UTM-tagged links let you see which post or Story drove a visit.

Give each campaign its own UTM name, such as utm_campaign=coast-launch, and judge posts by orders and revenue, not likes.

How do you keep tagged products accurate?

Keep stock and prices correct in Shopify, because the catalog syncs and a tagged product that turns out to be sold out or mispriced wastes the click and loses trust.

  • Track inventory for every product you tag, so sold-out variants show correctly.
  • After a sale ends, reset prices and compare-at prices so tags do not show an old offer.
  • Plan restocks for products you promote; our guide to out of stock and oversold products explains how.

FAQ

Do I need a business account to sell on Instagram with Shopify?

You need a professional account, which can be a business or creator account. It gives you insights and access to shopping features, which also depend on your country and Meta's current commerce policies.

Is the Facebook & Instagram sales channel free to add?

The channel itself is added from Shopify, but costs and requirements can change, so check the current details in the Shopify App Store listing and on Shopify's pricing page. Any ads you run are paid separately.

Why can't I tag products on Instagram yet?

Usually your shop is still under review, your account or country is not eligible, or your catalog has not synced. Check the status in the Facebook & Instagram channel in Shopify and Meta's current requirements; meanwhile, use a link in bio and Story link stickers.

How do I know which Instagram posts make sales?

Add UTM tags to every link you share, give each campaign its own name, then compare Instagram's insights with Shopify's sales by channel and referrer. Judge posts by orders and revenue, not likes.
