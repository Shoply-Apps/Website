TikTok marketing for Shopify stores: videos that bring sales

By the Shoply Apps team · 20 October 2026 · 9 min read

Key takeaways

  • TikTok marketing for Shopify works because short videos show a product working, which can reach new buyers even if you have few followers.
  • Set up a TikTok business account and connect it with Shopify's TikTok sales channel app; TikTok Shop is a separate in-app option whose availability and rules vary by country.
  • Hook viewers in the first seconds (problem first, result first, before and after, unboxing, myth vs fact) and film many angles of one product with a phone and natural light.
  • Send viewers to the right product or collection, tag your link with UTM parameters, and judge results by orders and cost per order against your margin, not views.
  • Check stock before pushing a product, because a video that sells it out costs you sales at the peak of demand.

TikTok marketing for Shopify stores means using short vertical videos to show your products working, then sending interested viewers to your store to buy. You don't need a studio or a big following. You need a product you can show, a phone, and a habit of posting and testing. This guide covers setup, video ideas and hooks, how to turn views into sales, and how to measure whether it pays.

Why does TikTok work for selling products?

TikTok works for products because short video lets you show what a product does instead of describing it. A 15-second clip of a stain coming out of a shirt explains more than a paragraph of copy, and viewers can judge size, texture and results with their own eyes.

TikTok also shows videos to people who don't follow you yet, based on what they watch. That means a new store with no audience can still reach buyers if a video holds attention. It also means results are uneven: some videos get few views, a few do much better, and you rarely know in advance which will be which. That is why the rest of this guide is about posting regularly and testing, not about one perfect video.

How do you set up TikTok marketing for Shopify?

Start with a TikTok business account, then connect it to Shopify through Shopify's TikTok sales channel app. A business account gives you analytics and business tools; the sales channel can sync your product catalog and set up tracking so TikTok can report on visits and purchases from your store.

  1. Create or switch to a business account in the TikTok app settings, using your store name and logo.
  2. Add the TikTok app from the Shopify App Store and follow its steps to connect your TikTok account and catalog.
  3. Check your product data first. A synced catalog uses your titles, images, prices and stock. Products with no image or a vague title look bad wherever they appear.
  4. Put your store link in your bio so every profile visit has a way to reach you.

TikTok Shop is TikTok's own in-app shopping feature, where people can buy without leaving the app. It is available in some countries only and has its own seller rules, approval and fees. Availability and features vary by country and change often, so check TikTok's and Shopify's current requirements for your country before you plan around it.

What makes a TikTok video sell a product?

The first few seconds decide whether anyone watches the rest, so every product video needs a hook: an opening line or image that gives viewers a reason to stay. After the hook, show the product doing its job clearly, then tell people where to get it.

Hook formulas that suit products, with examples for a lint remover:

Hook typeExample opening
Problem first"Your black coat is covered in fluff again."
Result firstShow the clean coat, then: "Here's how this took 30 seconds."
"Things nobody tells you""3 things nobody tells you about de-pilling a sweater."

Put the hook on screen as text too. Many people scroll with the sound off, and a caption tells them what the video is about at a glance.

What videos should you make for one product?

One product can carry many videos, because each one shows a different angle. Here are 10 ideas you can film for a single product:

  1. A quick demo showing the main thing it does.
  2. Before and after.
  3. Unboxing, from the package to first use.
  4. Three ways to use it.
  5. The problem it solves, acted out in 10 seconds.
  6. Answering a common question ("Does it work on wool?").
  7. Behind the scenes: packing orders or how it's made.
  8. A comparison with the "old way" of doing the job (without naming other brands).
  9. A customer's review or photo, with permission, read out or shown.
  10. Myth vs fact about the problem the product solves.

Not sure which product to start with? Pick one whose result is easy to see on camera, in stock and with a healthy margin.

How do you film TikTok videos on a budget?

A recent phone, natural light from a window and a clean background are enough. Film vertically, keep the product in the frame and close to the camera, and cut anything slow.

  • Captions on screen for the hook and key points.
  • Natural light facing the product, not behind it.
  • Sounds you have the rights to use. Business accounts usually have a narrower music library than personal accounts. Use TikTok's commercial sound library or audio you own, and check the platform's current rules.
  • Short and complete. End once the point is made.

How often should you post, and what should you test?

Post on a schedule you can keep, such as a few times a week, and keep going long enough to compare results. There is no fixed best frequency or posting time; test what works for your audience.

Change one thing at a time so you know what made the difference: try two hooks on the same demo, then a shorter and a longer cut, then a different format such as unboxing vs before-and-after. Keep a simple sheet of each video, its hook, length and results.

How do you turn TikTok views into Shopify sales?

Give viewers an easy path from the video to the right product page, and give them a reason to buy now. Views alone pay nothing; what matters is how many viewers click through and order.

  • Link in bio: point it to a collection of the products you feature, not your home page.
  • Product links and shopping features where your account and country allow them, so viewers can tap through from the video.
  • A clear offer: say what it is, who it's for and the price or deal in plain words.
  • The right landing page: the product page should match the video, load fast on a phone and show the same product. Our guide to increasing your Shopify conversion rate lists fixes worth doing first.

To measure, add UTM parameters to your bio link. UTM parameters are short tags added to a URL (for example ?utm_source=tiktok&utm_medium=social) that tell your analytics where a visit came from. Then check Shopify's reports for sessions and sales by traffic source or channel.

Use comments as content too. When someone asks a question, reply with a video that answers it. It's an easy next video idea, and it shows other viewers the product handling a real question.

In this example, two of the 10 videos brought most of the views, both using a before-and-after hook. That points the next two weeks at more before-and-after videos with different openings. If clicks were high but orders low, the problem would sit on the product page or the price, not the videos.

Revenue here is 6 × $32 = $192. Whether that is good depends on your margin after product cost, shipping and fees, which you should know before you spend anything on ads.

Should you work with creators or pay for TikTok ads?

Both can help once you know which products and messages work. Creators bring their own audience and style; ads let you push a proven video to more people.

With creators, start small: send products to a few creators whose audience matches your buyers, agree what they'll post and how you'll measure it, and make sure paid or gifted posts are disclosed as advertising, which is required in many countries. Our guide to influencer marketing for small Shopify stores covers finding, paying and measuring.

For ads, the lowest-risk start is boosting organic posts that already perform well, which TikTok offers through its ad tools (formats and names change, so check what's current). Use a small test budget and judge it by cost per order, not views:

Cost per order = Ad spend ÷ Orders from the ads

Example: you spend $60 boosting the best video and get 4 orders, so cost per order is $15. If each $32 order leaves you $13 after product cost, shipping and fees, you lose $2 per order on the ads. Either raise the price, find a cheaper way to reach buyers, or stop. Our guide to pricing for real profit margins shows how to work out that per-order profit.

What happens if a TikTok video goes viral and you run out of stock?

You lose sales at the exact moment demand peaks, and some buyers won't come back. Before you push a product, check its stock and how fast you can restock, and make sure the product page won't take orders you can't fill.

FAQ

Do I need TikTok Shop to sell on TikTok with Shopify?

No. You can post videos and send viewers to your Shopify store through the link in your bio and, where available, product links. TikTok Shop is a separate in-app shopping feature that is only offered in some countries and has its own rules, so check current requirements for your country.

How many videos should I post before judging results?

Post enough to compare, for example 10 videos over two weeks, and change one thing at a time such as the hook, length or format. One or two videos are not enough to tell what works for your product.

Can I use popular songs in TikTok videos for my store?

Business accounts usually have more limited music options than personal accounts. Use sounds from TikTok's commercial library or audio you own the rights to, and check the platform's current rules.

How do I know if TikTok ads are worth it?

Divide your ad spend by the orders the ads brought to get cost per order, then compare it with the profit each order leaves after product cost, shipping and fees. If cost per order is higher than that profit, the ads lose money.
